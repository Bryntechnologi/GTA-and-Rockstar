1961

Grand Theft Auto: London 1961

London, before the storm

A top-down crime spree in a city still rebuilding itself. Small crews, borrowed cars, and a lot of bad decisions.

The London expansion strips the series back to period cars, tight streets, and a pre-Beatles underworld. It is less a franchise milestone than a proof of concept: the same chase loop, transplanted into a colder city.

The London protagonist · London

1969

Grand Theft Auto: London 1969

Swinging London, wrong side of the river

The expansion moves the clock forward and the attitude up. British cars, British gangs, and a city that never quite looks away.

London 1969 leans into the visual language of the era — minis, mods, and a soundtrack of period pastiche. It remains one of the series' strangest side streets: a full GTA game built as an expansion.

The London protagonist · London

1984

Grand Theft Auto: Vice City Stories

Victor Vance arrives

A soldier returns to Vice City looking for a clean way out and finds a city that only trades in complications.

Victor Vance's story is a tragedy in slow motion: family loyalty, military connections, and a drug economy that turns every favour into a debt. The game ends with him alive but compromised, which makes the opening of Vice City land harder.

Victor Vance · Lance Vance · Vice City

1984

Grand Theft Auto: Vice City Stories

The Vance empire

Victor builds a criminal operation out of borrowed muscle and family obligation. Every new asset comes with a new enemy.

By the end of Vice City Stories, Victor controls territory, businesses, and a growing reputation. The cost is the relationship with his brother Lance and the knowledge that the city always collects. His death in the opening of Vice City is the punchline to this setup.

Victor Vance · Lance Vance · Vice City

1986

Grand Theft Auto: Vice City

Tommy Vercetti gets out

A prison sentence ends and a new one begins: a drug deal on the beach goes wrong, and Tommy has to improvise.

Tommy arrives in Vice City expecting a clean handoff. The deal is ambushed, the money disappears, and the people who sent him want answers. The game's opening is a masterclass in tone: sun, neon, and immediate violence.

Tommy Vercetti · Sonny Forelli · Lance Vance · Vice City

1986

Grand Theft Auto: Vice City

The Vercetti rise

Tommy works his way through the city's power brokers, buying property and collecting enemies in equal measure.

The middle act is an empire-building montage: clubs, businesses, assassination contracts, and a growing crew. Tommy's refusal to be a subordinate turns a debt into a takeover, and Vice City starts to feel like his city.

Tommy Vercetti · Lance Vance · Diaz · Vice City · Starfish Island

1986

Grand Theft Auto: Vice City

Lance Vance changes sides

The alliance at the centre of Tommy's rise starts to fracture. Loyalty in Vice City is always a temporary arrangement.

Lance's resentment and Sonny Forelli's pressure converge. Tommy's empire is strong enough to survive the city, but not strong enough to survive the people closest to it. The betrayal sets up the final confrontation.

Tommy Vercetti · Lance Vance · Sonny Forelli · Vice City

1986

Grand Theft Auto: Vice City

The last stand at the mansion

Sonny comes to collect, Lance makes his choice, and Tommy settles the debt with a very loud house call.

The finale is a siege: Tommy alone against Sonny's men, with Lance on the wrong side. When it ends, Tommy is the last one standing in a mansion that now belongs to him. The city is quiet for exactly one sunset.

Tommy Vercetti · Lance Vance · Sonny Forelli · Vice City · Starfish Island

1992

Grand Theft Auto: San Andreas

CJ comes home

Five years away, a funeral, and a neighbourhood that has moved on without him. Carl Johnson returns to Los Santos.

The opening of San Andreas is a homecoming story: CJ finds his mother dead, his family scattered, and the Grove Street Families losing ground. The game's first act is about rebuilding trust before it becomes about rebuilding an empire.

Carl Johnson · Sweet · Big Smoke · Ryder · Los Santos · Grove Street

1992

Grand Theft Auto: San Andreas

Grove Street vs the Ballas

Territory, drive-bys, and a family under pressure. The neighbourhood war escalates while the people closest to CJ make other plans.

The gang war is both personal and structural: the Ballas have backing, Grove has history, and the police treat the whole block as a problem to manage. CJ's loyalty is tested long before the bigger conspiracy surfaces.

Carl Johnson · Sweet · Big Smoke · Ryder · Los Santos · Grove Street

1992

Grand Theft Auto: San Andreas

San Fierro and the Loco Syndicate

The story widens beyond Los Santos: a new city, a new garage, and a drug network with ties back home.

San Fierro gives CJ a base, a cover business, and a target. The Loco Syndicate arc connects the street-level betrayal to a larger criminal supply chain, and introduces Mike Toreno as a very different kind of antagonist.

Carl Johnson · Mike Toreno · Wu Zi Mu · San Fierro

1992

Grand Theft Auto: San Andreas

The Caligula's heist

Las Venturas, a casino, and a plan that depends on too many people behaving predictably.

The Caligula's arc is San Andreas at its most cinematic: surveillance, preparation, and a heist that becomes a power struggle between criminal organisations. It is also where the game's three-city structure pays off.

Carl Johnson · Wu Zi Mu · Ken Rosenberg · Las Venturas · Caligula's Palace

1992

Grand Theft Auto: San Andreas

The homecoming

Los Santos riots, old friends return, and CJ finally gets to settle the score with the people who sold out Grove Street.

The final act ties the gang war, the police corruption, and the family betrayal together. CJ's return to Grove Street is not a victory lap; it is a cleanup. The last confrontation is personal, and the city keeps burning behind it.

Carl Johnson · Sweet · Big Smoke · Officer Tenpenny · Los Santos · Grove Street

1997

Grand Theft Auto

Liberty City, top-down

The original crime spree begins: steal cars, take jobs, and climb a ladder made of other people's mistakes.

The first GTA established the template: a nameless criminal, a city grid, and a radio dial. Liberty City is the starting point, but the game quickly moves through San Andreas and Vice City, treating each as a new playground.

The GTA protagonist · Liberty City · San Andreas · Vice City

1997

Grand Theft Auto

Three cities, one getaway

The original game's campaign spans multiple cities and ends in the kind of chase that defined the series.

GTA's structure was already episodic: jobs, betrayals, and a final run for freedom. The details are sparse by modern standards, but the rhythm — plan, drive, improvise — is recognisably the series' DNA.

The GTA protagonist · Liberty City · San Andreas · Vice City

1999

Grand Theft Auto 2

Anywhere City

A retro-futurist city, three gangs, and a protagonist with no name and no patience.

GTA2 leaned into dystopia: corporations, gangs, and a city that feels permanently on the edge of collapse. The game's reputation system and gang loyalty mechanics were early attempts at the living-world design the series would later refine.

Claude Speed · Anywhere City

2001

Grand Theft Auto III

Claude is betrayed

A bank job, a girlfriend with a gun, and a prison transport that does not go as planned. Liberty City goes 3D.

The opening of GTA III is still one of the series' cleanest setups: Catalina shoots Claude, leaves him for the police, and accidentally creates a free agent. The rest of the game is Claude climbing back through Liberty City's criminal hierarchy.

Claude · Catalina · Liberty City

2001

Grand Theft Auto III

Working for the families

Claude becomes useful to the Leone family, the Triads, and everyone else with a job that needs doing.

GTA III's middle act is a tour of Liberty City power: the Leone family, the Colombian Cartel, the Yakuza, and a media circus. Claude's silence makes him a perfect contractor and a complete mystery to everyone around him.

Claude · Salvatore Leone · Asuka Kasen · Liberty City · Staunton Island

2001

Grand Theft Auto III

The Cartel war

Catalina returns with a cartel, a hostage, and a very personal score to settle.

The Cartel arc turns the game's opening betrayal into a full-scale war. Asuka, the Yakuza, and the Colombian Cartel all collide, and Claude becomes the one variable nobody can control. The city gets louder and more dangerous.

Claude · Catalina · Asuka Kasen · Liberty City · Shoreside Vale

2001

Grand Theft Auto III

The exchange

A final deal, a final betrayal, and a Liberty City that keeps running long after the credits.

The ending of GTA III is deliberately cold: Claude survives, Catalina does not, and the city absorbs another story without changing its expression. It set the tone for every GTA ending that followed.

Claude · Catalina · Liberty City · Shoreside Vale

2008

Grand Theft Auto IV

Niko Bellic arrives

A war veteran comes to Liberty City chasing an old promise and finds his cousin's life was mostly a sales pitch.

Niko's arrival is the series' most grounded opening: a cramped apartment, a cousin with debts, and a city that offers work before it offers answers. The immigrant story gives GTA IV its emotional weight.

Niko Bellic · Roman Bellic · Liberty City · Hove Beach

2008

Grand Theft Auto IV

Blood and debts

Niko's search for the man who betrayed his unit pulls him into loan sharks, gangsters, and a war he did not start.

The middle of GTA IV is a web of obligation: Roman's gambling, Dimitri's manipulations, and the gradual revelation of what happened to Niko's squad. The city's optimism curdles into something colder.

Niko Bellic · Roman Bellic · Dimitri Rascalov · Liberty City · Broker · Bohan

2008

Grand Theft Auto IV

Deal or revenge

The story's final choice is not about money or loyalty. It is about whether Niko can live with either answer.

The Deal ending costs Roman his life; the Revenge ending costs Kate hers. GTA IV refuses a clean victory. Whichever path the player chooses, Liberty City keeps its ledger balanced and Niko keeps carrying the cost.

Niko Bellic · Roman Bellic · Kate McReary · Dimitri Rascalov · Liberty City

2008

Grand Theft Auto IV

The diamond deal

A bag of diamonds passes through too many hands, linking Niko's story to the two episodes that follow.

The diamond exchange is the connective tissue of the GTA IV era: a single botched deal that pulls in the Lost MC, Gay Tony's nightlife, and Niko's own circle. It is the series' clearest example of a shared criminal ecosystem.

Niko Bellic · Johnny Klebitz · Luis Lopez · Tony Prince · Liberty City

2009

Grand Theft Auto IV: The Lost and Damned

The Lost MC fractures

Johnny Klebitz tries to hold a motorcycle club together while its president pushes it toward a war it cannot win.

The Lost and Damned is a story about loyalty in a culture that mistakes loyalty for obedience. Johnny's attempts to keep the club solvent collide with Billy Grey's recklessness, and the fallout leaves the Lost weaker and angrier.

Johnny Klebitz · Billy Grey · Jim Fitzgerald · Liberty City · Alderney

2009

Grand Theft Auto IV: The Ballad of Gay Tony

Nightlife on the edge

Luis Lopez keeps Tony Prince's clubs running while the money, the drugs, and the Russians all want a piece.

The Ballad of Gay Tony is the flashiest of the GTA IV stories and the most anxious underneath. Luis is the competent one in a room full of people who have already spent the money. The finale ties back to the diamond deal and closes the IV-era trilogy.

Luis Lopez · Tony Prince · Ray Bulgarin · Liberty City · Algonquin

2013

Grand Theft Auto V

Three men, one city

Michael is bored, Franklin wants more, and Trevor is a live grenade with a driver's licence. Los Santos brings them together.

GTA V opens with a North Yankton heist and then jumps forward to a sunlit Los Santos where Michael's retirement is falling apart. Franklin's ambition and Trevor's chaos turn a midlife crisis into a criminal partnership.

Michael De Santa · Franklin Clinton · Trevor Philips · Los Santos · North Yankton

2013

Grand Theft Auto V

The first scores

A jewellery store, a motorbike, and a plan that works just well enough to attract bigger problems.

The early heists establish GTA V's structure: preparation, crew choice, and improvisation. Franklin's first jobs with Michael turn into a partnership, while Trevor's presence makes every plan less stable and more profitable.

Michael De Santa · Franklin Clinton · Trevor Philips · Lester Crest · Los Santos · Vinewood

2013

Grand Theft Auto V

Trevor finds out

The past catches up with Michael in the most volatile way possible: Trevor learns the truth about North Yankton.

Trevor's discovery reframes the entire story. Michael's retirement was built on a lie, and the reunion becomes a hostage situation with old friends. The game's tone shifts from satire to something more personal and dangerous.

Michael De Santa · Trevor Philips · Franklin Clinton · Los Santos · Sandy Shores · North Yankton

2013

Grand Theft Auto V

The agency years

The trio become assets for people with badges, budgets, and no intention of sharing the credit.

The FIB and Merryweather arcs turn the crew into deniable contractors. Every favour creates a new liability, and the line between criminal and government work becomes a joke the characters stop laughing at.

Michael De Santa · Franklin Clinton · Trevor Philips · Dave Norton · Steve Haines · Los Santos · Fort Zancudo

2013

Grand Theft Auto V

Three ways out

The final choice is a menu of betrayals: kill Trevor, kill Michael, or refuse the script entirely.

GTA V's three endings are a commentary on the series itself. Ending A and B kill a protagonist; Ending C lets the trio survive by turning on their handlers. The last option is the most satisfying and the least realistic, which is exactly the point.

Michael De Santa · Franklin Clinton · Trevor Philips · Los Santos

2026

Grand Theft Auto VI

Jason and Lucia

A new state, a new pair of protagonists, and a Bonnie-and-Clyde premise set against modern Leonida.

The revealed premise follows Jason and Lucia through a version of Vice City and the wider state of Leonida. The tone is modern, sunlit, and tense: a relationship story wrapped around a crime spree. Specific plot details are intentionally left out here rather than invented.

Jason · Lucia · Leonida · Vice City

2026

Grand Theft Auto VI

The state of Leonida

Causeways, wetlands, small towns, and a neon capital. The map is a state, not just a city.

Leonida expands the Vice City idea into a whole peninsula: Port Gellhorn's docks, Grassrivers' wetlands, Mount Kalaga's ridges, and the Keys' island roads. It is the broadest Rockstar map yet, and the setting is the story's co-star.

Jason · Lucia · Leonida · Vice City · Leonida Keys

2026

Grand Theft Auto VI

The next score

A partnership, a state full of opportunities, and the oldest question in the series: how much is enough?

The central tension of the revealed premise is trust: two people trying to build something together in a place designed to separate them. Everything else — the cars, the clubs, the coastline — is the scenery around that question.

Jason · Lucia · Leonida · Vice City

2026

Grand Theft Auto VI

A world that keeps moving

The series' next era is built around a living state: traffic, weather, wildlife, and a news cycle that never quite looks away.

Rockstar's pitch for GTA VI is density: a world with more systems, more interiors, and more ways for a plan to go sideways. The story is only one thread in a state that keeps generating its own.

Jason · Lucia · Leonida · Vice City
