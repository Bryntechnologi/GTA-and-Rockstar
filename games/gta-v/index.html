Lore files

location

Los Santos, 2013

Grand Theft Auto V splits its story across three protagonists and one sprawling satirical metropolis. Michael, Franklin, and Trevor move between heists, hobbies, and the entertainment industry's endless appetite for reinvention. The result is a West Coast playground built around planning, improvisation, and spectacle.

Story beats

2013 Three men, one city

Grand Theft Auto V

Michael is bored, Franklin wants more, and Trevor is a live grenade with a driver's licence. Los Santos brings them together.

GTA V opens with a North Yankton heist and then jumps forward to a sunlit Los Santos where Michael's retirement is falling apart. Franklin's ambition and Trevor's chaos turn a midlife crisis into a criminal partnership.

2013 The first scores

Grand Theft Auto V

A jewellery store, a motorbike, and a plan that works just well enough to attract bigger problems.

The early heists establish GTA V's structure: preparation, crew choice, and improvisation. Franklin's first jobs with Michael turn into a partnership, while Trevor's presence makes every plan less stable and more profitable.

2013 Trevor finds out

Grand Theft Auto V

The past catches up with Michael in the most volatile way possible: Trevor learns the truth about North Yankton.

Trevor's discovery reframes the entire story. Michael's retirement was built on a lie, and the reunion becomes a hostage situation with old friends. The game's tone shifts from satire to something more personal and dangerous.

2013 The agency years

Grand Theft Auto V

The trio become assets for people with badges, budgets, and no intention of sharing the credit.

The FIB and Merryweather arcs turn the crew into deniable contractors. Every favour creates a new liability, and the line between criminal and government work becomes a joke the characters stop laughing at.

2013 Three ways out

Grand Theft Auto V

The final choice is a menu of betrayals: kill Trevor, kill Michael, or refuse the script entirely.

GTA V's three endings are a commentary on the series itself. Ending A and B kill a protagonist; Ending C lets the trio survive by turning on their handlers. The last option is the most satisfying and the least realistic, which is exactly the point.
